    <h1 class="text-4xl sm:text-5xl font-display font-extrabold text-white mb-4 leading-tight">
      Spatial Pipeline: Photo Hotspot Engine
    </h1>
    <p class="text-lg text-slate-400 leading-relaxed font-sans max-w-3xl">
      <em>&ldquo;Where and when should I go to photograph elk, bighorn sheep or columbines?&rdquo;</em> <code class="text-cyan-300 font-mono">spatial_pipeline</code> pulls photographed observations from iNaturalist (or streams YFCC100M metadata), bins them into equal-area hexagons, counts <strong>unique photographers</strong> rather than raw uploads, and finds which subjects cluster in space (<strong>Moran&apos;s $I$</strong>), <em>where</em> their significant hotspots are (<strong>Getis-Ord $G_i^*$</strong>), and <em>when</em> they peak (<strong>seasonal &amp; diurnal lift</strong>).
    </p>

    {/* Section 1: Real-data validation */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">01/</span> What It Finds on Real Data
      </h2>
      <p>
        On one month of real Denver-area iNaturalist observations (<strong>June 2025: 6,417 photographed observations across 954 observers</strong>), the FDR-corrected Getis-Ord $G_i^*$ hotspots land where a local naturalist would send you:
      </p>
      <ul class="space-y-3.5 mt-4">
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Black-tailed prairie dogs</strong> form statistically significant hotspots on the <em>Rocky Mountain Arsenal National Wildlife Refuge</em>.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Mallards</strong> cluster along the <em>South Platte River corridor and city lakes</em>.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Pinewoods geranium</strong> clusters in the <em>western foothills</em>.</div></li>
      </ul>
      <p class="text-sm text-slate-400 pt-2">
        Earlier versions of this repository reported benchmark numbers measured on synthetic transcriptomics data; those synthetic benchmarks were removed when repurposing the engine around real geotagged photo and biodiversity datasets.
      </p>
    </div>

    {/* Section 2: Design choices */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">02/</span> Design Choices That Matter for Photo Data
      </h2>
      <p>
        Mapping raw photo counts mostly shows where people live and where roads go. Getting real habitat and landscape hotspots out of community data requires correcting for how people upload photos:
      </p>
      <ul class="space-y-3.5 mt-4">
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Photographer-weighted counts:</strong> each user counts at most once per subject per hexagon (<code class="text-cyan-300">(hex_id, label, user_id)</code>). Without this, one person who uploads 500 backyard shots creates a fake hotspot.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Equal-area projection:</strong> coordinates are projected from WGS84 to CONUS Albers (<code class="text-cyan-300">EPSG:5070</code>) before pointy-topped hexagonal binning, so every hexagon covers the same ground area and neighbor distances are in meters.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Share normalization:</strong> subjects are ranked by their <em>fraction</em> of each hexagon&apos;s photographers (<code class="text-cyan-300">normalize=&quot;share&quot;</code>), so a subject doesn&apos;t score high just because it appears where lots of people take photos.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">No isolated hexes in the statistics:</strong> hexagons without neighbors would inflate Moran&apos;s $I$ under binary weights ($N / S_0$ grows without adding neighbor pairs), so they are excluded from the statistical graph while still appearing on the map.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">No blurred locations:</strong> iNaturalist randomizes public coordinates for threatened species and privacy-restricted observations. Those records are excluded, along with any observation whose reported positional uncertainty exceeds <code class="text-cyan-300">1,000 m</code>.</div></li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    {/* Section 3: Mathematical formulation */}
    <div class="space-y-6">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">03/</span> Two Questions, Two Spatial Statistics + Temporal Lift
      </h2>

      {/* Moran's I & Getis-Ord Gi* */}
      <div class="bg-slate-950/80 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-slate-850 pb-2">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-cyan-400 uppercase tracking-wider">1. Global Moran&apos;s I (Which Subjects Cluster?) &amp; Local Getis-Ord Gi* (Where?)</span>
          <span class="text-[10px] font-mono text-slate-500">PER-SUBJECT BH-FDR</span>
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          For subject share $x_i = u_{i,\ell} / U_i$ across $N$ connected active hexagons with binary adjacency $A$, global <strong>Moran&apos;s $I$</strong> ranks which subjects cluster in space at all, while <strong>Local Getis-Ord $G_i^*$</strong> (with self-inclusive neighborhood $W = A + I$ and row sum $w_i = \sum_j w_{ij}$) flags the specific hexagons whose neighborhood has significantly more of that subject than expected under spatial randomness:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          I = \frac{N}{S_0} \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N a_{ij} (x_i - \bar{x})(x_j - \bar{x})}{\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \bar{x})^2}, \qquad G_i^* = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^N w_{ij} x_j - \bar{x} w_i}{s \sqrt{\frac{N w_i - w_i^2}{N - 1}}}
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          Because testing thousands of hexagons per subject means thousands of hypothesis tests, $G_i^*$ p-values are adjusted with vectorized <strong>Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (<code class="text-cyan-300">fdr_bh</code>)</strong> separately for each subject. Comparisons are among photographed hexagons only, so &ldquo;hot&rdquo; means hot relative to other places people shoot.
        </p>
      </div>

      {/* Timing Lift */}
      <div class="bg-slate-950/80 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-slate-850 pb-2">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-cyan-400 uppercase tracking-wider">2. Month &amp; Hour-of-Day Profiles with Lift (When to Shoot?)</span>
          <span class="text-[10px] font-mono text-slate-500">SEASONAL DEBIASING</span>
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          People are outdoors more in summer and at midday. To separate a genuine seasonal or diurnal pattern (such as elk in the September rut or crepuscular wildlife at dawn) from overall outdoor crowd bias, <code class="text-cyan-300">compute_timing</code> counts unique photographers per <code class="text-slate-300">(subject, month)</code> and <code class="text-slate-300">(subject, hour)</code> and divides by the baseline share across all photos:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          \text{Lift}(\ell, b) = \frac{\text{share}(\ell, b)}{\text{share}(\text{all photos}, b)}
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          Timestamps at exactly <code class="text-cyan-300">00:00:00</code> (date-only observations or cameras without a clock set) count toward month profiles but are excluded from hour-of-day profiles.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>

    {/* Section 4: How it's built */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">04/</span> How It&apos;s Built &amp; Verified
      </h2>
      <ul class="space-y-3.5 mt-4">
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Resumable, polite fetchers (<code class="text-cyan-300">inat.py</code>, <code class="text-cyan-300">flickr.py</code>):</strong> iNaturalist&apos;s standard paging caps at 10,000 results, so <code class="text-cyan-300">fetch-inat</code> pages by observation ID (<code class="text-cyan-300">id_above</code>) at ~1 req/s with a query-tied Parquet page cache (<code class="text-cyan-300">.inat_cache/</code>) and a per-run request budget. <code class="text-cyan-300">ingest-yfcc</code> streams the 65.6 GB YFCC100M SQLite or <code class="text-cyan-300">.bz2</code> TSV in chunks.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Interactive Streamlit + deck.gl map (<code class="text-cyan-300">app.py</code>):</strong> filter by month (<em>When</em>) or taxonomic group (birds, mammals, plants, insects), click any hexagon to inspect its top subjects (★ = significant $G_i^*$ hotspot), view Creative Commons thumbnails with photographer attribution, or share a spot via URL (<code class="text-cyan-300">?hex=563</code>).</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">General-purpose 2D spatial engine underneath:</strong> <code class="text-cyan-300">binning.py</code>, <code class="text-cyan-300">graphs.py</code> and <code class="text-cyan-300">out_of_core.py</code> stay domain-agnostic—supporting square, hexagonal, KD-tree, quadtree and Voronoi binning, halo-exchanged out-of-core tiling, Dask chunked binning, and optional RAPIDS <code class="text-cyan-300">cuML</code> GPU graph construction.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-cyan-500 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Verification suite:</strong> $G_i^*$ is checked against a direct one-hexagon-at-a-time implementation of Ord &amp; Getis (1995), <code class="text-cyan-300">fdr_bh</code> is checked against <code class="text-cyan-300">scipy.stats.false_discovery_control</code>, and ingestion is tested against a realistic Colorado YFCC fixture (<code class="text-cyan-300">tests/yfcc_fixture.py</code>) covering edge cases like prolific uploaders, videos, missing geotags and low-accuracy points.</div></li>
      </ul>
    </div>
