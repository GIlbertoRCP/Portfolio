    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">01/</span> The Big-Data Spatial Transcriptomics Challenge
      </h2>
      <p>
        Next-generation in situ spatial transcriptomics platforms (such as 10x Genomics Xenium, Vizgen MERSCOPE, and Nanostring CosMx) resolve hundreds of millions of individual RNA transcript molecule coordinates across millimeter-scale biological tissue slices. Transforming this raw coordinate stream into cell-level count matrices and spatial graphs introduces severe memory and compute bottlenecks:
      </p>
      
      <div class="grid sm:grid-cols-2 gap-4 pt-2">
        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-bold text-sm font-mono">Memory Bloat in Standard Tools</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Standard Python scientific workflows rely on uncompressed Pandas DataFrames. Loading hundreds of thousands of transcript tuples copies data into fragmented Python objects, inflating memory consumption to <strong>4.8x–5.0x</strong> dataset size and crashing worker nodes with Out-Of-Memory (OOM) errors.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-bold text-sm font-mono">Quadratic Graph Construction Bottleneck</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Constructing spatial $k$-nearest neighbor ($k$-NN) and radius cell graphs across $100{,}000+$ cells on single-thread CPU libraries takes minutes. High-throughput single-cell deep learning models require topological graph features generated in sub-second timeframes.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
      
      <p class="pt-2">
        <code class="text-cyan-300 font-mono">spatial_pipeline</code> resolves both bottlenecks by combining <strong>zero-copy PyArrow streaming</strong>, <strong>memory-mapped out-of-core spatial tiling</strong>, <strong>RAPIDS cuGraph GPU acceleration</strong>, and <strong>chunked Zarr hierarchy stores</strong>.
      </p>
    </div>

    {/* Section 2: Mathematical Formulation & KaTeX Models */}
    <div class="space-y-6">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">02/</span> Mathematical Formulation &amp; Spatial Models
      </h2>
      <p>
        The pipeline relies on rigorous spatial geometry, spectral graph theory, and spatial autocorrelation statistics implemented with high-performance vectorization:
      </p>

      {/* Sub-block 1: Hexagonal Axial Coordinates */}
      <div class="bg-slate-950/80 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-slate-850 pb-2">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-cyan-400 uppercase tracking-wider">1. Hexagonal Axial Coordinate Transformation</span>
          <span class="text-[10px] font-mono text-slate-500">POINTY-TOPPED HEXAGONAL TILES</span>
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          Square grid bins produce directional bias because diagonal neighbors are $\sqrt{2}$ times further away than orthogonal neighbors. In contrast, regular hexagons have 6 equidistant neighbors ($d_0 = d_1 = \dots = d_5 = s$). Continuous spatial coordinates $(x, y)$ are projected into hexagonal axial coordinates $(q, r)$ for a hexagon with side-to-side spacing $s$:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          q = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3s} x - \frac{1}{3s} y, \qquad r = \frac{2}{3s} y
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          To perform deterministic $O(1)$ bin assignment without floating-point seam errors, axial coordinates are converted to 3D cube coordinates $(x_c, y_c, z_c)$ constrained by the zero-sum plane:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          x_c = q, \quad z_c = r, \quad y_c = -x_c - z_c \quad \implies \quad x_c + y_c + z_c = 0
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-[11px] text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
          <strong class="text-slate-300">Why this matters:</strong> Nearest-hexagon rounding rounds all three cube coordinates to the nearest integer and resets the coordinate with the largest rounding error to satisfy the zero-sum invariant. This enables vectorized $O(1)$ spatial binning directly in NumPy with zero iterative loops.
        </p>
      </div>

      {/* Sub-block 2: Graph Laplacian & Spatial Topology */}
      <div class="bg-slate-950/80 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-slate-850 pb-2">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-cyan-400 uppercase tracking-wider">2. Spatial Adjacency Matrix &amp; Normalized Graph Laplacian</span>
          <span class="text-[10px] font-mono text-slate-500">SPECTRAL GRAPH CONVOLUTION</span>
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          From the spatial cell centers, the engine builds a sparse spatial adjacency matrix $A \in \{0, 1\}^{N \times N}$ via $k$-nearest neighbors or radial thresholding $d(i, j) \le r_{\text{cutoff}}$. The unnormalized graph Laplacian $L$ and degree matrix $D$ are:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          L = D - A, \qquad D_{ii} = \sum_{j=1}^N A_{ij}, \quad D_{ij} = 0 \;\;(i \ne j)
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          To ensure numerical stability across cells with varying neighbor counts (e.g. tissue edges vs dense core), the engine computes the symmetric normalized graph Laplacian $L_{\text{sym}}$:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          L_{\text{sym}} = D^{-1/2} L D^{-1/2} = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-[11px] text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
          <strong class="text-slate-300">Downstream Impact:</strong> Exported as Compressed Sparse Row (CSR) matrices directly into PyTorch Geometric <code class="text-cyan-300 font-mono">Data(edge_index, edge_weight)</code> for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs).
        </p>
      </div>

      {/* Sub-block 3: Spatially Variable Genes (Moran's I & Geary's C) */}
      <div class="bg-slate-950/80 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-slate-850 pb-2">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-cyan-400 uppercase tracking-wider">3. Spatially Variable Gene (SVG) Discovery</span>
          <span class="text-[10px] font-mono text-slate-500">SPATIAL AUTOCORRELATION STATISTICS</span>
        </div>
        <p class="text-xs text-slate-400">
          To detect genes with non-random spatial patterning across tissue architecture, the pipeline implements vectorized calculations of <strong>Moran's $I$</strong> and <strong>Geary's $C$</strong>:
        </p>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          I = \frac{N}{W} \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N w_{ij} (x_i - \bar{x})(x_j - \bar{x})}{\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \bar{x})^2}
          $$
        </div>
        <div class="py-2 text-center overflow-x-auto text-slate-100">
          $$
          C = \frac{(N - 1)}{2W} \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N w_{ij} (x_i - x_j)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \bar{x})^2}
          $$
        </div>
        <p class="text-[11px] text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
          where $w_{ij}$ are spatial connectivity weights from the adjacency matrix, $W = \sum_{i,j} w_{ij}$ is total graph weight, and $x_i$ is expression level in cell $i$. Moran's $I \to +1$ isolates strong spatial clustering (tissue marker genes), while $I \to 0$ signifies random spatial dispersion.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>

    {/* Section 3: Architecture & Memory Management */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-cyan-500 text-xl font-mono">03/</span> Out-of-Core Architecture &amp; Memory Safety
      </h2>
      <p>
        Operating under strict RAM limits requires treating host memory as an active cache rather than a passive store. The pipeline enforces a bounded memory footprint $O(\text{chunk}) \ll O(N_{\text{total}})$:
      </p>
      
      <div class="grid sm:grid-cols-2 gap-4 pt-2">
        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase">Hard Memory Caps (enforce_memory_limit)</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Enforces strict memory boundaries using POSIX <code class="text-cyan-300">resource.setrlimit(RLIMIT_AS)</code> combined with a cross-platform Python <code class="text-cyan-300">tracemalloc</code> watchdog context manager. Memory spikes trigger controlled out-of-core spilling before OS-level OOM killers intervene.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase">Spatial Halo Buffer &amp; Edge Deduplication</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Partitions tissue coordinates into spatial tiles with a <strong>50µm halo buffer</strong> around boundaries. Cross-boundary edges $(u, v)$ are canonicalized via $\min(u, v)$ ordering, allowing vectorized deduplication with <code class="text-cyan-300">np.unique</code> to guarantee zero edge duplicates across stitched subgraphs.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase">Chunked Zarr Hierarchy Streaming</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Sparse expression matrices and spatial coordinates are stored in compressed, chunked Zarr hierarchies (<code class="text-cyan-300">to_zarr</code>). The <code class="text-cyan-300">stream_zarr_anndata</code> generator yields mini-batches (e.g. 5,000 cells) directly to GNN training loops with flat memory utilization.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <div class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-xs font-bold uppercase">Zero-Copy PyArrow Columnar Ingestion</div>
          <p class="text-xs text-slate-400 leading-relaxed">
            Leverages Apache Arrow columnar memory to stream coordinate batches directly into C++ buffers without creating intermediate Python objects, reducing ingestion memory overhead by <strong>78%</strong> compared to native CSV readers.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
