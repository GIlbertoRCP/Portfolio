      Predictive SDN

    <p class="text-lg text-slate-400 leading-relaxed font-sans max-w-3xl">
      Most networks react to congestion after it happens. The idea here was to predict it a few steps early and reroute traffic first. An LSTM forecasts congestion from switch telemetry, and a reinforcement-learning agent decides how often to poll the switches so the controller isn't flooded with stats.
    </p>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-4">

  <aside class="mb-16 border border-amber-500/30 bg-amber-950/20 rounded-2xl p-6 space-y-2">
    <p class="text-xs font-mono font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-400">Postscript: the forecaster didn't beat a baseline</p>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-300 leading-relaxed">
      The original evaluation reported F1 ≈ 0.99 for the congestion forecaster. When I revisited it, "the next step is congested if this step is" scored F1 0.993 on the same data. A shuffled split of overlapping windows had hidden that. With a chronological split, a 3-step moving average beats the model. I turned those checks into a tool and used it on published results.
    </p>
