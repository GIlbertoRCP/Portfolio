      A solo project: I trained a 3D humanoid in MuJoCo with PPO for 160 million steps to walk over obstacles and down a steep ramp. Most of the work was reward shaping: every penalty I added to fix one bad behavior tended to create a new one.
    </p>

    {/* Section 1: Setup */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-emerald-400 text-xl font-mono">01/</span> The Setup
      </h2>
      <ul class="space-y-3.5">
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-emerald-400 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">A lighter body.</strong> MuJoCo's default humanoid is bulky, so I thinned its limbs and made its legs less dense. That let it move its legs faster and fall forward in a more controlled way, which is most of what walking is.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-emerald-400 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Eyes on the ground ahead.</strong> Without sensing, a policy can just memorize one track. I attached five rangefinder rays to the torso that fan out vertically to scan the path ahead, and wrote an observation wrapper (<code class="text-emerald-300">VisionObservationWrapper</code>) adds their distances to what the policy sees, so it has to react to the terrain.</div></li>
        <li class="flex items-start gap-3"><span class="text-emerald-400 mt-1">✓</span><div><strong class="text-white">Slow, steady PPO.</strong> A small learning rate (5e-5) and large batches (10,240 steps) kept training stable. I ran training in 10–15M-step chunks so I could check each stage and adjust the reward before continuing.</div></li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    {/* Section 2: Failure modes */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-emerald-400 text-xl font-mono">02/</span> Things It Learned That I Didn't Want
      </h2>
      <p>
        Early on, each reward change fixed one problem and exposed another:
      </p>
      <div class="grid sm:grid-cols-3 gap-4 pt-2">
        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-white">Hot-stove paralysis</h3>
          <p class="text-sm text-slate-400">I penalized joint movement too hard, so the cheapest option was to not move at all and just collapse.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-white">The drunken crab</h3>
          <p class="text-sm text-slate-400">Walking sideways turned out to be more stable than walking forward. Adding a penalty on sideways speed fixed it.</p>
        </div>
        <div class="bg-slate-950 border border-slate-850 rounded-xl p-5 space-y-2">
          <h3 class="text-sm font-bold text-white">Diving to quit</h3>
          <p class="text-sm text-slate-400">When I set a big penalty for falling, it learned to dive into the ground right away, ending the episode before more penalties piled up.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>

    {/* Section 3: Penguin slide */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-emerald-400 text-xl font-mono">03/</span> The Penguin Slide
      </h2>
      <p>
        Later in training it found its own way down the steepest ramp: instead of stepping down, it drops to its knees and slides. That's a local optimum. It gets the reward, just not the way I intended.
      </p>
      <p>
        I tried penalizing the slide, and it didn't work. Performance dropped sharply, because the policy couldn't unlearn the slide without also forgetting how to walk. It stayed in.
      </p>
    </div>

    {/* Section 4: Limits */}
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-display font-extrabold text-white flex items-center gap-2">
        <span class="text-emerald-400 text-xl font-mono">04/</span> What It Can't Do Yet
      </h2>
      <p>
        It can't get back up. After the slide, or any fall, the episode is effectively over. The fix I'd try is a hierarchical setup: a separate lower-level policy that only learns to stand up, which the walking policy hands control to after a fall.
      </p>
    </div>
